Tools and methods we use

  • Pipe wrench

    Used to grip and turn threaded pipe and fittings. Essential for supply line work and valve replacement, especially on older galvanized or brass connections.

  • Drain snake

    Clears clogs in sink, shower, and toilet drains. We run the correct size cable for the line so we clear the blockage without damaging the pipe walls.

  • Hydro jetting machine

    Blasts grease, scale, and root intrusion out of larger drain lines. We use it on main sewer lines and commercial kitchen drains where a snake alone won't clear the buildup.

  • Leak detection equipment

    Finds hidden leaks under slabs and behind walls without tearing the house apart. We use acoustic and pressure testing to pinpoint the source before we cut anything open.

  • Soldering torch and pipe cutter

    Used for copper pipe repairs. We cut the damaged section clean and solder in a new piece with proper fittings for a joint that holds under full pressure.

  • Pressure gauge

    Measures water pressure at fixtures and at the main line. It tells us whether low pressure is a supply problem, a fixture problem, or a hidden leak.

  • Uniform Plumbing Code compliance

    Every repair we make follows the Uniform Plumbing Code and Florida Building Code. That means correct pipe sizing, proper slope on drain lines, and approved materials. It matters for inspections, for insurance, and for the next person who works on your system.

  • Pressure testing after repair

    Once a supply line repair is complete, we restore water and check the joint under full house pressure. We watch it for several minutes with a dry rag to catch any weep before we call the job done.

When to call for plumbing repairs

  • You notice water stains spreading on the ceiling below an upstairs bathroom.

    We trace the leak to its source, which is often a failed wax ring, a cracked drain shoe, or a loose supply connection. We repair the fitting or replace the fixture seal, then test with a full flush and running water. The stain stops growing, the ceiling dries out, and you avoid a full drywall replacement.

  • Your water heater is making popping sounds and the water isn't as hot as it used to be.

    We inspect the tank, check the heating elements or burner, and test the anode rod. If sediment buildup is the issue, we flush the tank. If the unit has failed, we quote a replacement from Rheem or Bradford White. You get consistent hot water back, and you know whether the unit has years left or needs replacing.

  • Your kitchen sink drains slowly and backs up every few weeks no matter what you pour down it.

    We run a drain snake to clear the immediate clog, then inspect with a camera to check for grease buildup, root intrusion, or a collapsed section of pipe. If the line is damaged, we quote the repair. The drain flows freely, and you know whether the problem is a one-time clog or a line that needs repair.

  • You're selling your home and the inspector flagged a corroded shutoff valve under the sink.

    We replace the valve with a new quarter-turn ball valve, check the supply lines, and test for leaks. The work is code-correct and ready for re-inspection. The inspection item clears, and the buyer's lender signs off without delay.

Repair or replace: what you should know

Not every plumbing problem needs a full replacement. Knowing the difference saves you money and avoids unnecessary work.

  • When repair works

    A single failed cartridge, flapper, or valve is a repair job. The fixture body is still solid, so replacing the worn part restores full function at a fraction of replacement cost.

  • When replacement wins

    If the fixture body is cracked, corroded through, or the repair cost approaches half the price of a new unit, replacement makes more sense. We tell you which side you're on.

  • When to call us

    Water where it shouldn't be, a meter that spins with everything off, or a drain that backs up repeatedly all point to a problem that won't fix itself. Call before it gets worse.
